# v14 acoustic construction and radiation
Isolated experimental branch. v13 and production untouched. v14 graphs opt into new geometry and radiation; v13 regression retains its exact original path.
APT capacity is editable log range: finite zero or approximately 10 nJ–1 J; sustained zero or approximately 10 µW–100 W. V scales physical capacity, actual delivered work still depends on acceleration, duration, mass and reactions. Acceleration slider logarithmic 0.1–5000 m/s². Time unchanged. No automatic per-instrument gain. Pressure full-scale remains 2 Pa.
Exposed vibrating area is separate from volume: shell side surface, sheet broad face, filament lateral surface, fluid opening area. Thin hollow envelopes with comparable transverse dimensions select reduced circumferential/axial shell modes; shell mass comes from wall area and thickness, not a filled plate. Sheet modes use a simply supported rectangular flexural/tension approximation. Explicit fluid opening neck/volume can create a Helmholtz estimate. None is a full 3D shell/plate/air solver. Radiation uses a mode-weighted impedance port with compact-source cancellation, mechanically accounts for radiated work, and maps radiated power to approximate far-field pressure at a 0.1–2 m pickup distance. Orientation/distance affect reception, not mechanical radiation. Multiple radiators are summed coherently as an approximation; no calibrated microphone or exact directivity.
Examples: GUITAR loads pick, string, M palm, bridge, soundboard, cavity air and output. BELL and DRUM load editable curved hollow metal shell and tensioned sheet; violin with bridge/body/air tested offline. Body is represented by soundboard plus air volume, not a complete side/back/bracing mesh. The matched bare/body comparison uses identical source and string settings; actual delivered work can differ due to loading. No flute jet or true violin stick-slip introduced. Tests log measured acoustic levels and work/radiation, assert energy closure and distance invariance; they do not certify timbre. WAVs and CSVs exported at the same fixed scale.
